The original pedestrian handrail

More than half a century above the Golden Gate.

The steel used in our work was part of the Golden Gate Bridge's original pedestrian handrail. Milled by Bethlehem Steel Company in Pennsylvania, it was installed during construction and remained on the bridge from 1937 until the handrail replacement in 1993.

Look closely

The steel people walked beside for generations.

For more than fifty years, this handrail bordered the bridge's pedestrian walkway above San Francisco Bay. Its surface, fasteners, seams and markings are part of the material's history.

Those details matter because they connect a finished piece directly to the structure it came from.

The handrail tells part of its own story through the fasteners. This short video looks closely at the railing on the bridge and shows the difference between the rounded rivets and later hex-head bolts.

How the story became ours

In 1993, one section of bridge steel changed everything.

When I learned that the Golden Gate Bridge's original pedestrian handrail was being removed, I bought a 12½-foot section weighing roughly 1,000 pounds. I wasn't starting a business. I wanted a piece of the bridge for myself.

I cut a 115-pound section from that rail and made it into a headboard. Friends saw it and began asking if I could make pieces for them. What started as a personal project became something much larger.

In 1994, I bought the remaining handrail steel that was available, left my job, and founded Golden Gate Furniture Co. The same historic material has been the foundation of our work ever since.

Preserving the material

The bridge remains part of every piece.

Today, the original handrail steel is worked in our San Francisco metal studio. It is cut, cleaned, sandblasted and finished individually.

Some pieces retain the marks and character accumulated over decades. Others are finished in International Orange, the color inseparable from the Golden Gate Bridge.

We don't try to erase the steel's history. That history is the reason the material matters.
